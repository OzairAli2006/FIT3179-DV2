Subject Choices in Senior Secondary School

What students study across Australia, with a closer look at Queensland.

Which subjects are most popular, and how have enrolments changed over time? This visualisation explores senior secondary subject enrolments across Australia and compares male and female participation. It then looks at individual subjects across Queensland schools and education regions.

01

What are students choosing?

Some learning areas attract more students than others. The national picture for 2024 shows which areas are most popular, where male and female participation differs, and which languages students study most.

What stands out?

English leads at 79.5%, followed by Mathematics at 67.0%. Female participation is higher in six of the eight learning areas, while male participation is higher in Mathematics and Technology. Japanese, French and Chinese together account for 58.6% of the language enrolments shown.

Figures cover tertiary recognised subjects that can contribute towards university admission. Students may appear in several learning areas, so participation percentages do not add to 100%. The treemap shows percentages of language enrolments.

Source: ACARA - Year 12 Subject Enrolments .

02

How have subject choices changed?

Subject choices change over time. Looking at enrolments across several years shows which learning areas have gained or lost participation, whether gender gaps have changed, and how language rankings have shifted.

What stands out?

Participation is lower in 2024 than in 2010 across all eight learning areas. Arts records the largest decrease, from 29.2% to 20.7%, a fall of 8.5 percentage points. Female participation remains higher in Arts, while male participation remains higher in Technology and Mathematics. Japanese, French and Chinese occupy the top three language positions throughout the period.

The heatmap shows female minus male participation in percentage points (pp). Positive values mean higher female participation, negative values mean higher male participation.

Source: ACARA - Year 12 Subject Enrolments .

03

A closer look at Queensland

Broad learning areas tell only part of the story. A closer look at Queensland's individual subjects shows which have the most enrolments and how male and female enrolments differ across selected subjects.

What stands out?

English has the most Unit 4 enrolments at 27,822, followed by General Mathematics at 21,381. Essential English and Essential Mathematics each have around 20,000 enrolments. Biology records more female than male enrolments, while Physics and Sport and recreation record more male enrolments.

These figures show enrolments in Unit 4, the final unit of a senior subject, for the 2025 completion year. They count students enrolled in each subject, not students who passed. Students taking several subjects are counted once in each subject.

Source: QCAA - Senior Subject Enrolment Data .

04

Does geography reveal another divide?

Large enrolment totals do not always mean a subject makes up a larger proportion of local study. These maps explore where subjects are studied across Queensland and compare regional totals with subject proportions. Choose a subject using the dropdown below each map.

What stands out?

Many schools with recorded enrolments are located along the south east coast and around regional centres. With Biology selected, Metropolitan North and Metropolitan South have the largest regional enrolment totals. North Queensland has the highest Biology proportion. A region with more enrolments does not necessarily have a higher subject proportion.

Dots show school sites with recorded enrolments. Two schools without matched coordinates are omitted from the dot map. Boundaries are simplified, with offshore islands omitted and Norfolk Island excluded.

Subject proportion is the selected subjects enrolments divided by all recorded Unit 4 subject enrolments in the region, multiplied by 100. This includes subjects outside the dropdown and measures enrolments, not individual students. The colour scale changes with the selected subject.

Sources: QCAA - Senior Subject Enrolment Data by School ; Queensland Government - Education Region Boundaries ; Queensland Schools Directory ; Queensland School Locations ; Archived School Details .
